The first draft is a map, not the territory

Why I stopped polishing chapter one before chapter two existed.

Ada Quill
Nov 02, 2026

For three years I rewrote the opening of my second novel. Every morning I sat down with coffee and the same twelve pages, and every evening I had twelve slightly different pages that were no closer to a book.

The problem was never the sentences. The problem was that I was trying to describe a country I had not visited yet. A first draft is a map you draw while walking, and nobody draws an accurate coastline from the harbour.

So I made a rule. No sentence gets polished until the chapter after it exists. It felt reckless for about a week, and then it felt like breathing. The book I had been circling for three years took eleven months once I let the map stay rough.

If you are stuck on page one, try writing the last page instead. You will learn more about your opening from the ending than from the fortieth rewrite of the first paragraph, and the ending will forgive you for being messy.

The rough map is not a failure of craft. It is the craft, done in the right order, and the polish you skipped will still be waiting when you finally know where the roads go.
