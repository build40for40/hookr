Notes from the slush pile

What a year of reading other people's openings taught me about mine.

Last year I volunteered to read submissions for a small literary magazine. I expected to learn about other writers. Mostly I learned about the habits I had stopped noticing in my own pages.

The openings that worked never explained themselves. They started in the middle of a decision, with a person who wanted something, and they trusted me to catch up. The ones that failed spent a page clearing their throat.

I started printing my own first pages and reading them in the same chair, at the same hour, with the same tired eyes. It was humbling. My throat clearing took two pages, not one, and it was very well punctuated.

The fix was not clever. I cut everything before the first moment a character chose something, and then I put back only what the reader could not live without. Usually that was one line, sometimes none at all.

If you read for a magazine, you will become a better editor of your own work within a month. If you cannot, read your opening aloud to someone who owes you nothing and watch where their eyes drift away.
