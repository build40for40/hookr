On reading your own book in public

Posted on 3 November 2026 by Ada Quill

The first time I read from my own book in public, I discovered that I had written several sentences that could not be said aloud by a human being with ordinary lungs. On the page they looked elegant. In a bookshop on a wet Tuesday they sounded like a dare.

I went home and read the whole novel aloud to my kitchen. It took four evenings and most of a pot of honey. Every place I stumbled, I marked with a pencil, and by the end there were more pencil marks than chapters. Not one of them was a typo. They were all rhythm.

Here is what I learned. A sentence that cannot be read aloud is usually a sentence that does not know what it wants. It is carrying two ideas and pretending they are one, or it is hiding the verb at the end like a present nobody asked for. Split it, and both halves get better.

The second lesson was about silence. Audiences do not need every gap filled. When I stopped rushing from the end of one paragraph to the start of the next, people leaned in. The pause did the work that I had been trying to do with adjectives, and it did it for free.

Now I read every chapter aloud before it leaves my desk. It is slow, and my neighbours have opinions, but the books are better for it. If you write, try it once with the chapter you are proudest of. You will find out quickly whether it is proud of you back.
